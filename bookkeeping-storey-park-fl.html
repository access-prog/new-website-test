Who actually does business in Storey Park

Storey Park opened in 2015 on land that had been farm and wetland, and Lennar kept a lot of the green rather than filling every lot. The result is a community of ponds and trails where a good share of the working population never leaves the neighbourhood on a Tuesday.

That is not an accident of character. Fiber internet came standard here, along with the smart-home wiring the builder put in every house. A community built with real bandwidth from day one attracts people whose businesses run on it: online sellers, remote consultants, agencies with clients in three time zones, video and course producers, IT contractors. Their revenue arrives through Stripe and PayPal and their expenses are forty small software subscriptions.

The second group is younger than in the older Orlando neighbourhoods, because the houses are. Storey Park sold to first-time and second-time buyers, and a decent number of them started something in the years since. A side business that pays for itself turns into an LLC, then into a business with a payroll, usually without anyone stopping to fix the books along the way.

Then there is the retail at the edge. The Shoppes at Storey Park where Dowden Road meets 417 is the Publix-anchored centre serving this community, Randal Park and Meridian Parks. Small tenants in a centre like that run on thin margins and file sales tax, and the ones that survive are the ones who know their numbers monthly rather than annually.

Why Storey Park owners hand off the books

The first thing that brings Storey Park owners in is the CDD. This community sits inside its own Community Development District, and that assessment shows up on the tax bill next to the county taxes as though it were the same kind of item. It is not. Part of a CDD assessment retires the bonds that paid for the infrastructure and part covers ongoing operations and maintenance, and the two are treated differently. For an owner-occupier the deductible portion is narrower than the line on the bill suggests. For anyone renting a Storey Park house out, the treatment changes again. Getting this wrong is quiet, and it compounds every year it repeats.

The second is solar. Storey Park was built as Orlando's first solar-powered community, with rooftop panels on the houses covering a serious share of household electricity. A residential system has its own credit rules and its own basis questions, and the moment a room in that house becomes a home office, the allocation between personal and business use stops being theoretical. Owners who guessed at the split usually guessed generously.

The third is the shape of an online business. Payment processors do not hand you bookkeeping. Stripe deposits arrive net of fees, which means the deposit in the bank is not the revenue, and a business that books the deposit as income understates both its sales and its expenses. Refunds, chargebacks and platform fees each need their own account or the profit and loss statement is fiction.

The fourth is simply that the business outgrew the spreadsheet and nobody noticed the day it happened.

A CDD assessment is not a property tax, and the year somebody books it as one is the year the return stops matching the tax bill.

Quarterly taxes, 1099s, and the January scramble in Storey Park

Florida has no personal income tax. The filing calendar still exists and it is unforgiving about dates.

A Storey Park business with employees files the RT-6 reemployment tax return each quarter with the Florida Department of Revenue. A business selling anything taxable files the DR-15 sales and use tax return monthly or quarterly depending on what it collects. Online sellers get caught by that second one more than anyone, because a storefront on a marketplace can create a filing obligation the owner never consciously took on.

The January problem here is contractor payments. An agency or a course business typically pays editors, designers, virtual assistants and a bookkeeper before it hires anybody, and every unincorporated one of them who took six hundred dollars or more during the year is owed a 1099. Those payments are usually spread across a card, PayPal and a couple of direct transfers, with a W-9 on file for none of them.

Collect the W-9 first, tag the vendor when the payment is set up, and January is an afternoon. Skip it and January is a fortnight of chasing people who worked for you once in March.

Messy QuickBooks in Storey Park? Fix it before it compounds

The cleanup pattern in a community this young is specific: the file is not old, it is unreconciled.

A business started in 2021 or 2022 connected its bank feed, ran clean for a few months, then hit its first busy season. Three years later the feed has been importing unattended the whole time. Uncategorized Expense is holding hundreds of transactions, a Stripe payout is booked as a single lump of income with the fees buried inside it, and the owner's personal card is in there too because it was easier at the time.

We rebuild from statements rather than from the feed, because the feed is the thing that drifted. Payouts get split into gross sales and fees. Personal spending comes out to owner's draw where it belongs. When it is done, the profit and loss statement matches what the business actually did, which for an online seller is often the first time the margin has been visible.

Frequently asked questions about bookkeeping in Storey Park

Are my Storey Park CDD assessments tax deductible?

Partly, and the split matters. A CDD assessment usually has a debt service portion retiring the district's bonds and an operations and maintenance portion. They are not treated alike, and neither is simply a property tax. On a rental property the treatment differs again. We work it out from your actual assessment rather than from the total on the bill.

Can I claim the solar on my Storey Park house against my business?

The residential clean energy credit is a personal credit and belongs on the personal return. If part of the house is a genuine home office, an allocation question opens up, and the honest answer usually gives you less than you hoped. The square footage used for business is what drives it, not the share of the electricity the panels cover.

I sell online from Storey Park. Do I owe Florida sales tax?

It depends on what you sell, where the buyer is, and whether a marketplace is collecting on your behalf. Plenty of Orlando online sellers have a DR-15 obligation they never registered for, and plenty of others are registered and filing zero returns they do not need. Both are fixable, and both are cheaper to fix before a notice arrives. This is how we handle it for Storey Park owners throughout Orange County.

My Stripe deposits do not match my sales. Is that a bookkeeping problem?

Yes, and a common one. Stripe pays you net of its fees, so the deposit is smaller than the sale. If the deposit gets booked as income, your revenue is understated and your fee expense has disappeared entirely. The fix is to split every payout into gross sales and fees, which also makes your real margin visible.
